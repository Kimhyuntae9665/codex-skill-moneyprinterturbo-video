# Shot production: world first, action second, camera third

## Default company-story direction (2026-09-06)

Read `easy-company-stories.md` first. The current company-story default is easy business storytelling, not engineering education. The technical examples below are conditional implementation references. For contracts or business power, direct customers, orders, investments, invoices, shops and competitors through changing situations; do not inherit the cooling plate, packet journey or chip board simply because its renderer exists. Before image/3D work, pass the easy-topic and observed-social-response selection in the entrypoint. Camera/material quality cannot rescue an inaccessible topic.

## Story correspondence before route selection

Follow `story-aligned-miniatures.md` for the current NVIDIA-v2-based miniature direction and character craft. Before route selection, make `story-visual-map.md`. Each core claim needs identifiable actors/roles and an observable relationship or state change in the actual shot. A generic worker, invoice or server-room image is not sufficient just because its noun appears in the narration. If an action can be replaced by arbitrary office footage without changing the explanation, the explanation may exist only in audio/captions; reboard it. Preserve valid multi-shot events rather than forcing every relationship into one frame.

## Route selection

The user's current main-film default is Blender 3D rendered on the configured remote GPU. Design simple business stories as dimensional scenes with actual subject actions. The generative-video alternatives below are optional supporting routes; they do not supersede this default. See `blender-company-default.md`. Easy narration is not a reason to replace the 3D film with flat illustration.

Only after the story correspondence gate, use text-to-video for a justified context or visually independent supporting insert. An insert cannot stand in for a missing central relationship. Use image-to-video when the object, worker, or geometry must persist. Use a last-frame continuation only when the accepted endpoint is a good starting composition. Do not carry a deformed hand, false port, occluding lid or accumulated text artifact into the next shot. Reset to the approved world reference after drift; camera continuity alone is not enough.

For geometry-critical explanations, use a tested 3D animation or a verified simplified schematic for the decisive mechanism. Generated footage can establish atmosphere and scale, but it must not contradict the accurate insert. Keep these modes visually compatible through materials, orientation and lighting. A view of a pretty cooling plate with disconnected channels cannot support a continuous-flow claim.

## Creative development before storyboard lock

The user found the NVIDIA board film underdeveloped at the idea stage, not merely short on camera variety. Allocate an explicit creative pass before coding or final TTS. Produce a compact `visual-treatment.md` within the production project; it is a decision aid, not a claim of rendered footage or audience-tested success. The following steps are the director's own work and do not require additional agents.

1. **Open the design space.** Write three distinct visual treatments for the same supported story. Each gets a hook image/event, the chain of visible actions and an ending reveal. Change the narrative viewpoint or staging logic: following a packet through a constraint, watching a system assembled around an arriving chip, or comparing two clearly illustrative situations. Three versions of a board flyover do not qualify. Use the subject's own physical or commercial relationships; do not default every company to chips, roads or factories.
2. **Explore the decisive beats.** For the hook, main mechanism and ending, propose at least two different visible events. Use a compact idea bank when stuck: interrupted action, conceal/reveal, constrained passage, cause traveling between spaces, empty-to-active state, reversible assembly, scale reveal, or a callback whose meaning has changed. These are prompts for invention, not a required sequence of effects. Select combinations that explain the claim without inventing facts.
3. **Choose with reasons.** Compare the treatments in a few sentences for curiosity, clarity without narration, compositional progression and implementation cost. Name the strongest feature and weakest point of each. Adopt the best coherent route or combine compatible beats. A familiar simple treatment may win if its action communicates more clearly; asset reuse alone is not a creative justification.
4. **Write an event-based storyboard.** For each semantic beat record: the viewer's current question, visible starting situation, action/reveal, new understanding, composition/scale, transition motivated by the event, narration purpose, factual boundary and production method. Camera movement is a supporting field. `Show the board from another angle while explaining the next point` is an unresolved beat, not an approved scenario.
5. **Review as a first-time viewer.** Read only the event column and inspect rough thumbnails at phone size. Ask: What changed? Why should I watch the next beat? Is this new information visible or only spoken? Can I distinguish adjacent frames without reading their captions? Does the ending repay the opening question? If the answers repeat, redesign or merge the beat before rendering. Log concrete weaknesses and the changes made, not a generic self-awarded creativity score.
6. **Prove the chosen idea cheaply.** Make rough compositions and a short animatic of the hardest causal transition before expensive final footage. Preserve time for the viewer to notice the reveal. A written plan alone cannot pass visual comprehension. If a shot is impractical, preserve the intended event in a simpler staging; do not replace the entire story with one static set and repeated orbits.

Develop the rough script and visual treatment together. Once the event sequence works, finalize narration, trim it and lock the timeline. Do not freeze a long narration first and then scramble to cover it with repeated footage. Existing reusable assets should serve the chosen storyboard and may be combined with sourced real imagery, original 3D, cutaways or clear diagrams when each medium has a specific explanatory job.

Keep continuity through an identifiable subject, causal connection, material language or motivated match cut, not compulsory reuse of the same backdrop. Variety must help comprehension: random scene changes, excessive transitions and new props without a story role are also weak direction. Change location or scale when the explanation moves there. Allow a strong action to breathe instead of imposing a fixed cut interval.

**NVIDIA example of genuine alternatives, not a new factual claim:**

- Packet journey: begin with a waiting compute task, trace its missing input back to a queue, then reveal the interconnect and the partner relationship.
- System assembly: introduce a sourced partner identity and a labeled ASIC; supporting components arrive with clear roles, revealing why one chip is only part of the system.
- Changed meaning: begin with an apparently standalone chip, reveal its surrounding connections in successive layers, and return to the chip with the ecosystem now visible.

All three need source-aware qualifiers and a visible payoff. Choose and adapt one rather than making every episode follow this example. Detailed constraints and the concrete Fusion reboard below remain useful implementation references.

passage inside a server cold plate. CONTEXT: the same dark green circuit board

Record model, generation duration, seed if supported, reference files, cost, take number, acceptance result and selected in/out. An 8-second take may yield only 2–4 useful seconds. This is an editing option demonstrated in a third-party tutorial, not a guaranteed generation yield or universal target.

## Six reusable shot operators

For current company-story production, select the business-compatible operators and contact/completion rules in [causal-staging-library.md](causal-staging-library.md). The six operators below remain implementation options. Record the chosen information layer and the event that finishes the beat in the existing storyboard; camera distance alone is not an information layer. A money transfer needs identifiable sender, recipient and transaction type; a service scene needs identifiable user, provider and supplied result. Prototype the decisive contact/transfer before polishing the set.

| Operator | Start → action → end | Camera purpose | Reject when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exterior to interior | intact housing → shell removed → working part | access hidden layer | shell still blocks the part |
| Exploded assembly | assembled stack → parts separate along axis → reconnect | reveal relationships | parts swap order or intersect |
| Tracer route | token enters known port → follows real connection → exits | follow causality | teleport or wrong direction |
| Counterfactual | plausible design → load/constraint activates → visible failure | test intuition | fake historical event implied |
| Capacity transformation | empty bays → authorized activation → bottleneck moves | show economic result | lit racks imply unsupported revenue |
| Callback wide | familiar exterior → pull back from explained interior | changed meaning | unrelated cinematic ending |

In a generative shot, request one operator first. Add a second beat only after proving reliable output. In deterministic animation, key cameras and objects from the same scene clock. Avoid unbounded fluids, destruction or crowd simulation when a controlled material/geometry change conveys the same mechanism.

## Company identity and meaningful labels

At a partner's first substantive mention, show its sourced logo, readable company name and short role together. For an NVIDIA–Marvell story, an example is the Marvell logo beside `맞춤형 칩 협력`, followed by the relevant chip and connection action. This is an editorial role illustration, not an authentic Marvell chip photograph or proof of shipped hardware. Add the logo in compositing, record source/usage terms, preserve its proportions and clear space, and do not ask a generator to invent it. If an authentic usable logo cannot be obtained, use the exact company name and record the asset gap rather than silently substituting a generic icon. Keep role labels and connections clear so adjacency does not imply ownership or endorsement.

Replace renderer placeholders with viewer-facing component names before render approval. In a custom AI ASIC explanation use `ASIC` and, on first appearance, `맞춤형 AI 칩`. Use `XPU`, `CPU` or the specific product name when that is what the evidence actually describes; do not globally rename all non-NVIDIA chips ASIC. Company, component category and commercial role are separate labels. Technical words should identify something the viewer can see or understand, not decorate the frame.

## Visual progression acceptance criteria

The 2026-09-05 NVIDIA Fusion review exposed a practical failure: ten camera views of the same board were still experienced as one screen. Keeping a persistent subject means following the same causal question through meaningful changes, not locking every shot to a board on a table.

For a typical 30–60 second company story, plan at least three distinct explanatory compositions as a working target: context/actor and role, mechanism/constraint close-up, and changed system or business relationship. A continuous scene can satisfy this if it genuinely transforms, opens hidden space or reveals a new causal layer. Camera orbit, crop, zoom, new caption or lighting color alone do not count as another composition. Longer pieces need additional explanation, not repeated angles to fill duration.

Before full rendering, make a storyboard strip and describe for each beat: what new thing becomes visible, what changes in the object/system, and what the viewer now understands. Merge or rewrite consecutive beats with the same answer. If more than half the runtime is essentially the unchanged layout with camera/caption updates, reboard it or explicitly justify a demonstrably evolving continuous action. This is an editorial review, not something shot count or a JSON validator can certify.

Add content only when a verified fact supports a new visual action or consequence: a partner brings a chip, a connection accepts it, or supporting components enter the system. Do not add unrelated facts or invent contracts, capacity or revenue merely to create more scenery. If added explanation no longer fits the edited voice, extend the runtime or split the story rather than accelerate it.

### Bottleneck: show the cause and the downstream cost

Use a readable sequence: arrivals continue → a labeled constrained transfer point passes fewer packets → the queue visibly grows upstream → a downstream compute task pauses waiting for input. On the first reveal, show `전송 병목` and `데이터 대기` with unobstructed anchors. Make the waiting task/state legible through a paused work cycle or clearly labeled activity indicator, not an unexplained color change. Show these changes in sync with narration, long enough to recognize them.

If showing an improved path, hold the illustrative workload and framing comparable, then show the queue draining and the task resuming. Do not imply that a named technology removes every bottleneck. Ratios, throughput counters and speed multipliers require measured sources; otherwise label the sequence `원리 설명용 예시` and use qualitative changes. Symbolic packets stay on defined paths without teleporting through walls. Review the action once with narration muted: can the viewer locate the constraint and see who is waiting? If not, reject/rework the mechanism shot before the full render.

## Character construction

For a recurring worker, generate an original reference sheet with front, three-quarter and working-hands views. Freeze jacket, gloves, tool, hair and build. Pick one appearance to use as the actual image reference; a multi-panel sheet can accidentally produce split-screen video if fed without cropping/selection. Use image tools for image edits.

Use a role-first shot: hands turn a fitting → connector closes → camera follows the connected hose. A character looking at the camera without affecting the mechanism adds little. Historical characters may be reconstructed in suitable clothing; don't present generated faces as authentic footage. Human anatomy and tool contact need explicit frame review.

## Audio and edit clock

1. Finalize pronounceable Korean script and factual qualifiers.
2. Generate phrase-level voice. Keep original audio and timing metadata.
3. Trim only verified silence, preserving phoneme edges; inspect waveforms around each edit. A -35 dB full-mix analysis of a reference cannot tell how its isolated voice was edited.
4. Build the edited audio timeline and captions from that timeline.
5. Choose footage in/out or retime modestly to fit each semantic span. If large retiming harms action, regenerate or rewrite. Do not slow every clip to fill an arbitrary total.
6. Place effects at the visible contact/change. Mix generated ambience separately from replacement narration.

Use question → short causal answer → newly visible consequence as the rhythm. Maintain conversational certainty about established facts, and explicit uncertainty where warranted. Dramatic delivery does not require shouting or indiscriminate acceleration.

## Caption and annotation layout

At 1080×1920, start caption tests around 54–68% of frame height with a bold font and dark outline. This is an adjustable composition range, not a fixed channel measurement. Keep labels away from the operating part and Shorts UI. Prefer one short phrase over long paragraph captions; split at meaning, not arbitrary character counts.

Put proof text in the edit, not the generated image. If a number changes, include the period and denominator in a similarly readable context line. Use tracked anchors for arrows and dimensions; test both the arrow's arrival and its final state. Avoid putting an arrowhead under the number it explains.

## Generation budget

Estimate attempts, not only finished seconds:

`generation cost = sum(takes per shot × credits per take)`

Then report voice, music, upscale and rerender costs separately. Check current UI before spending. A tutorial's old per-video price excludes failed takes and may use a different model or subscription. Set a maximum retry count and stop/replan when the cause of failure persists. Keep all useful takes for editing; discard only by recorded decision, not blind overwrite.
