# Cinematic Mechanism Shorts

Make the viewer say “이 회사가 이렇게 돈을 버는 거였어?” without having to study. Default to an entertaining company story about money, contracts, customer dependence, competition, distribution or a surprising decision. Preserve cinematic storytelling—visible events, recognizable actors, reversals and payoff—using original wording and imagery.

The 2026-09-06 editorial correction supersedes engineering-first examples in the older research notes. Do not select optics, chip internals, networking architecture, cooling or similar engineering lessons merely because a renderer can show them. Technical explanation is an opt-in mode requiring an explicit technical request. “Make an Amazon story” does not mean “explain chip design.”

**Production default, confirmed by the user on 2026-09-06: Blender 3D + the configured remote GPU.** Easy content does not mean flat visuals. Keep everyday company stories and stage them in dimensional offices, shops, meeting rooms and other relevant spaces with visible actions. Use 2D for captions, sourced logos and concise supporting diagrams, not as the main-film substitute unless the user requests that change. Read [references/blender-company-default.md](references/blender-company-default.md) before production. This correction takes precedence over earlier language treating illustrated 2D as an equivalent default.

**Latest direction, 2026-09-06: develop the user-selected NVIDIA v2 into polished miniature storytelling.** The subsequent photoreal Amazon film was rejected because its pictures did not show the narrated business relationships. This supersedes the miniature ban and the “accepted-looking photoreal frame first” rule. Preserve NVIDIA v2's recognizable subjects, connections, partner roles and expanding system view; improve assets, purposeful motion, composition and consistent miniature people. Do not copy chips into an unrelated company story.

**Non-negotiable order:** evidence-correct relationship → visible story event → readable continuity → coherent visual craft → technical delivery. A beautiful office, server corridor or walking person does not demonstrate who invests in whom or who buys whose service. Read [references/story-aligned-miniatures.md](references/story-aligned-miniatures.md) before any storyboard or repair. It owns the story-to-picture contract, miniature people/sets, NVIDIA baseline and the corrected Amazon example. Photoreal production is conditional on a future explicit brief and must pass the same story gate.

## 1. Select an easy story before selecting a renderer

Compare several candidate business stories on ordinary-viewer comprehension, surprise/conflict, observed audience response, visual events and source support. Capture actual likes, reposts, replies, views, publication date and observation time when visible; missing counts are null/unavailable, not zero. Read relevant replies to distinguish curiosity from jokes, outrage or factual rejection. Reactions are evidence of interest, not truth or guaranteed Korean Shorts performance.

The ease gate comes first: explain the candidate in one conversational sentence without specialist knowledge; identify who wants what, who pays whom and the surprising outcome. Reject a candidate whose appeal requires several technical definitions. A viral engineering post can fail this brief. A quiet but easy, surprising story can beat a generic announcement; state the reason.

Before committing, write the hook and final payoff in plain Korean. If connecting them requires a lecture, change the topic. Deliver `topic-selection.md` with finalists, actual response evidence, rejection reasons and the chosen one-sentence story. Editorial scores are judgments, not measured retention.

## 2. Establish the production route for the selected story

The default is authored Blender 3D rendered on the user's configured remote GPU. Choose scenes for the story, not a story for available hardware assets. Stage business events: an investment arrives, a customer signs, a bill is issued, a rival enters, a shop or queue changes. Do not force a server-board cutaway into a contract story. Sourced imagery and 2D graphics support the 3D film. Preserve and assess any explicitly requested realistic-reference fidelity separately.

Current measured 8GB-GPU route for exact physical action (2026-09-05): authored 3D. ControlNet, SVD and FramePack did not pass the tested mechanical action/support requirements; see `references/gpu-quality.md`. This is not a requirement that every company story use engineering 3D. Reuse installed tools; new model experiments must address a visible need in the selected story.

Record the available video generator, authenticated session, usable budget, renderer, voice provider, resolution, and what has actually been tested.

Before an expensive model run, encode and decode a tiny synthetic clip with the actual environment's writer. A working model import does not validate a media API that may have been removed. Preserve decoded frames or a recoverable intermediate before export so an encoder failure does not discard the completed generation. Record this as technical preflight, not visual approval.

Before a full render, budget storage from actual representative frame bytes × total frames, plus models, repair passes, encoding and free-space reserve. Lossless PNG compression can reduce storage; verify decoded pixel identity before replacing completed frames, and never recompress a frame while it is being rendered or repaired. Follow the storage procedure in `references/remote-gpu.md`.

- **Default main film:** use sufficiently detailed Blender 3D sets, purposeful object/character action, material depth, contact shadows and motivated changes of space. Render using the verified remote GPU. Generated video is optional supporting material, not required or a reason to download more models.
- **Mechanism proof:** deterministic Blender or another tested renderer proves topology, cameras and timing. A schematic test can pass implementation while still failing visual similarity.
- **2D support and fallback:** use 2D/2.5D for abstractions, captions and previews. If remote access fails, keep making the Blender scene, audio and local previews; report the actual connection status and retry the configured route. Do not silently replace the requested 3D main film with 2D. A main-film medium change needs the user's choice after concrete progress and the actual remaining obstacle are shown.

Do not make a whole video before proving the hardest reveal and one causal action in a short clip. Use a bounded retry budget appropriate to the shot; after repeated failure change the shot plan, not merely the seed. Purchases, paid subscriptions, or cloud-GPU charges need the applicable user authorization. A login obstacle does not stop local research, prompts, voice, blockout, or documentation.

Spend available GPU capacity on a controlled comparison before declaring a rendering limitation: one unchanged scene at preview/final samples, one deliberate material/lighting improvement, then a short motion check. A higher sample count does not repair weak composition or invented mechanics. Retain the accepted setting and measured cost; do not increase resolution or download larger models without identifying a visible defect those changes should solve.

## 3. Turn the selected company story into visible events

Choose an everyday paradox with stakes: a company pays someone who becomes its customer, a rival depends on its infrastructure, or a marketplace earns before sellers profit. Keep the verified factual spine separate from the dramatic title. The business story must remain understandable if chip-model names are removed.

Useful narrative families:

1. familiar company → unexpected deal → who gets what → money/customer relationship revealed → business payoff;
2. follow a customer, purchase, investment or invoice → surprising second payment → who controls the route → meaningful limitation;
3. bold decision or sourced market chatter → what people think → what documents show → why the company becomes powerful or vulnerable.

These are options, not a mandatory seven-beat checklist. Do not invent a failed historical attempt to fill a beat. An illustrative wrong model must be visibly hypothetical.

### Visual story laboratory (mandatory before lock)

Treat the storyboard as a sequence of viewer questions, not a list of camera angles. For each beat write: what the viewer currently thinks, what constraint or revelation changes that understanding, what changes on screen, and why the next beat follows. When a plausible wrong explanation helps the story, demonstrate it as a clearly hypothetical counterfactual before showing the actual mechanism; do not force a failure into every topic. A beat is only considered different when it changes the represented space, state, scale, information layer, or visible action.

Choose from a representation palette: a recognizable place, contract desk, customer counter, purchase/invoice journey, contrasting company roles, queue or consequence/callback. Technical macro and cutaway scenes belong to an explicitly technical brief. Keep a returning hero recognizable while changing what it reveals. Give the central event enough time to recognize cause and result; a 0.5-second accent is suitable only when no new relationship must be learned.

Use the 12-film study as an action vocabulary, not a fixed template: pick the core narrative verb and stage its identifiable start, contact/transfer or change, and readable outcome. Change information scale when the viewer needs a new answer; carry an actor/object identity across the cut. Where useful, show an intervention and its remaining limitation in the same visual world, then return to the opening subject with changed meaning. Select only the relevant operators in `causal-staging-library.md`; do not add engineering content, disaster imagery or extra runtime just to imitate the reference.

Before locking the script or building the scene, develop and compare three genuinely different visual story treatments. For the hook, central mechanism and payoff, explore at least two ways to stage the event—not merely two camera positions. Select for viewer curiosity, visible causality, evolving composition and achievable production. Follow `Creative development before storyboard lock` in [references/shot-production.md](references/shot-production.md). Do not start with the easiest existing asset and force every sentence onto it. Jointly develop narration and visible events; edited audio locks timing only after the visual storyline is viable.

For one easy reveal, start around 40–65 seconds and stop when the payoff lands. Do not fill time with engineering background, a second thesis or obligatory valuation. Expand only when the story needs and earns it. Linked stories may use the `wallstreet-edge-shorts` dossier budget or be split. Never accelerate speech to rescue an overfilled script.

## 4. Freeze evidence and the visual world

Write a short claim ledger with source URL, date, exact scope, fact/inference/rumor status and visual implication. Generated pictures are illustrations, never documentary proof. Dimensions, dates, percentages and contract terms come from sources and are composited accurately after generation.

For company stories, use `wallstreet-edge-shorts` for X/Reddit discovery and financial boundaries. Research may be broad; the final narrative must remain coherent. Sourced reporting, market chatter and plausible interpretations can be the main attraction, not merely an appendix, without waiting for official confirmation. Read [references/market-chatter-direction.md](references/market-chatter-direction.md) when the user wants a spicy or rumor-led story: keep source status legible while preserving direct, energetic narration. Do not turn a disputed or unverified claim into an established fact. P/E and stock recommendations belong only when requested by the editorial brief.

Create a world sheet: hero object, invariant geometry, entry/exit ports, important connections, scale cue, setting, material palette, lighting and camera axis. Preserve the topology that carries the explanation; harmless background details need not be identical across every cut.

Classify visible objects as physical, symbolic, or intentionally exploded. For physical props, name their support/attachment and check its visible plausibility across every camera, including the opening/background. Do not place emissive mesh strips as ceiling fixtures without a ceiling/mount or convincing suspension. Studio lights may illuminate from outside the frame without visible fixtures. Exploded parts and symbolic tracers need clear explanatory treatment; this exception does not excuse floating ordinary furniture or equipment.

Characters use a consistent miniature design and perform an identifiable story role; follow the character/action checks in `story-aligned-miniatures.md`. They may be customers, counterparties, workers, witnesses or scale references. Use a simple original character sheet only when recurring identity matters. Keep clothing, tool, handedness and task stable; prefer hands/over-shoulder/wide inserts over unnecessary face close-ups. Do not claim that the reference used a particular character model, LoRA or voice clone without direct evidence.

## 5. Edit conversational narration before final picture timing

Use natural Korean questions, short clauses and concrete verbs: “돈을 넣었는데, 그 회사가 다시 손님이 됩니다.” Prefer an everyday relationship before a term. Usually one unfamiliar term and two principal companies suffice for one reveal. Read the script without visuals: it must make sense during casual listening. Remove background that delays the surprise. Say the one qualifier that changes the story, such as promised spending versus money already earned, instead of hiding it in tiny footnotes.

Generate and trim narration by semantic phrase; preserve consonants and intentional pauses. The user's established 1.30× TTS preference is a starting setting, not a universal perceived speed. Verify the actual audio duration and comprehension. ASR syllables per full runtime are a density proxy, not a precise speaking-speed measurement.

Use the edited audio as the timeline clock. Align new objects with nouns, actions with verbs, and state changes with results. Cut at a phrase boundary or an intentional semantic accent; don't force a cut every two seconds. Avoid an automatic 0.5-second silence before every number.

## 6. Generate a shot library, then select usable ranges

Each shot must specify: purpose, start state, story-relevant action, end state, camera purpose, narration span, invariants, source claim, model/renderer, and rejection conditions. Also maintain `story-visual-map.md` linking each core claim to visible actors/roles, relationship, observable change and actual review frames. Do not accept filled-in fields as proof that the rendered scene conveys them.

For the shots that carry the hook or relationship reveal, extend that existing map with the chosen reference facet and its Blender implementation: actor/blocking IDs, screen direction, camera start/end and lens/FOV, focus target, visible reaction and protected portrait action area. Camera movement must reveal information or follow an event; film-camera brand names in a prompt do not implement lighting or motion. Inspect the adjacent cut pair for gaze, hand/prop state and exposure continuity. Reframe camera/coverage first when adapting a landscape reference to 9:16; change actor blocking only deliberately and without changing the relationship. See the direction reference for examples; do not create a separate checklist document per field.

- Establish the object in the opening moving frame; export a separate thumbnail instead of imposing a frozen logo intro.
- Select spatial access to answer the next question: context wide, approach, component macro, cutaway/exploded view, flow or consequence wide. These are options, not a fixed ladder every episode must traverse.
- Camera motion alone does not count as a mechanism. An action counts only when it changes the relationship or state described by the narration. Opening a server-room door is not a substitute for demonstrating AWS supplying a named customer. Assembly, routing or transfer must visibly serve the claim.
- Preserve the causal subject, not one unchanging set. A company explainer should move between distinct explanatory compositions (for example: partner/role introduction, close-up constraint, system-level consequence). Count new visible actions and information, not camera angles; compare a storyboard strip before full rendering. Follow the visual progression acceptance criteria in `references/shot-production.md`.
- Require a storyboard strip with a `mode` and `state_change` column. Flag three consecutive cells sharing the same representation mode without a state change for editorial review; merge/reboard repetition, while retaining a demonstrably informative continuous action. This is not an automated visual-quality pass. Prefer transitions motivated by the narration's noun or verb: a door opens when the script says open, a queue grows when demand exceeds capacity, and a map route changes when the destination changes.
- For every major claim, compare an everyday visual metaphor with a literal rendering of the sourced relationship, then choose the clearer one. A company story may use a contract, customer or invoice as its literal evidence illustration; it does not require a mechanical cutaway. In an explicitly technical bottleneck story, show source → queue → blocked downstream node → workaround → released flow and accurate component names. If a logo or real product image is used, introduce it at the matching role and keep the claim source separate from the illustration.
- Generate one action with one camera instruction first. Multi-shot generation is optional after a reliable single-shot proof.
- Generation length and final cut length differ. Keep the useful middle of a longer generated clip; do not fill narration with its defective lead-in or tail.
- Inspect transitions as well as stills: ports jumping sides, tool penetration, disappearing supports and reversed flow break the explanation.

## 7. Compose graphics and sound around the story

Before production, apply [references/action-rhythm-and-polish.md](references/action-rhythm-and-polish.md). Give the opening a story-linked event within about 1.5 seconds and select 2–3 major actions with distinct preparation, fast execution, contact, settle and readable outcome. Record shared action timestamps for animation, camera/focus and sound in `action-cues.json`; do not distribute accents at arbitrary percentages. Use purposeful changes of shot scale, selective DoF, material-aware motion and narration-driven music ducking. Effects, lens numbers, shakes and bass drops are choices, not universal obligations. Inspect a 10–15 second edited motion/sound proof covering the hook and decisive action before full rendering; report playback and listening separately.

Use short Korean captions in a stable phone-readable band selected to avoid the action. The reference often places them around the lower-middle, not at the bottom edge; adapt to each scene and platform UI. Post-compose text, dates, dimensions and arrows. Never preserve generated gibberish as technical evidence.

Keep the object dominant. An arrow needs a visible head, an unambiguous origin/destination, sufficient contrast and clear space around labels. Use one accent for one role; disclose symbolic particles/heat maps when they might be mistaken for literal objects.

When a named company materially enters the story, actively source its real logo and introduce its role at the matching narration beat; the protagonist's watermark alone is insufficient. Use meaningful component names instead of generic placeholders: for a sourced custom AI ASIC story, show `ASIC` with `맞춤형 AI 칩`, not merely `CUSTOM`. Preserve the actual product category when ASIC, XPU and CPU are not interchangeable. A bottleneck must visibly connect queue buildup to downstream waiting; color changes alone cannot establish it.

Sound cues follow visible action. Lower background music under narration and remove generated speech when replacing it with the Korean master. Never claim a final listening review when only waveform or codec checks ran.

## 8. Verify and deliver

Run `scripts/validate_shots.py path/to/shot-plan.json` for the structural contract, then inspect actual video. Passing the JSON contract does not certify visual quality.

Review the full screen before the hero detail: background → middle distance → foreground, corners, upper edge, floor contact, then action. Include first frame, each camera's entry/exit, every cut pair, and continuous motion review where feasible. Named contact tests such as `scripts/audit_environment.py` cover only declared objects; they do not certify every object or replace whole-scene visual inspection. Record user-found misses and revise an earlier pass when it proved too broad.

Deliver MP4, clean thumbnail, topic-selection comparison, actual social-response records, source/claim summary, shot plan, prompts or reproducible source, and an honest QA record. Separate `story_alignment`, `miniature_craft`, `character_action`, `technical`, `visual sampled`, `full playback`, `listening`, and `reference similarity` results. Failed story alignment blocks editorial acceptance even when other categories pass. A local proof is useful even when commercial generative-video access remains unavailable, but it is not a finished reference-quality episode.

For a company-led brief, put the properly sourced company logo and one concise representative line on the separate thumbnail. Keep the opening moving scene focused on the story instead of forcing a frozen logo introduction.

For marketing iteration, compare several original hooks while holding the explanation quality stable. Use channel analytics after publishing if supplied; public views alone cannot reveal retention or prove which technique caused success.

For a new production, explicitly carry these three gates into its shot/QA record: (1) reusable assets have support/port metadata and whole-frame checks; (2) any ControlNet/SVD route is marked planned, tested or accepted, with no physics guarantee inferred from conditioning; (3) normal-speed playback/listening and any audience experiment have separate evidence/status. Change only one opening variable per A/B comparison; absent real analytics, label the result a hypothesis rather than measured retention improvement. Track authoring and render cost separately.
