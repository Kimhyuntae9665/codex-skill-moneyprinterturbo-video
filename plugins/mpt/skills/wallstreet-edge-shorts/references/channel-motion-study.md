## What works

### The title and first image state a broken intuition

The opening does not introduce the subject academically. It shows an apparently impossible condition: broken elevator ropes without a fall, a cable built in the air rather than a factory, an offset bridge pier, a column built underwater, or a basement that was not excavated in the expected order.

The first frame already contains the physical subject. There is no separate logo slate or generic scene before the question.

### One object survives several shots

The bridge cable, pier, elevator, or foundation remains the protagonist while the camera and material state change. The sequence moves through wide view, close-up, cutaway, process, and comparison without losing spatial identity. This continuity makes rapid cuts easier to follow.

### The animation answers the question

The strongest scenes do more than decorate narration:

- a cable becomes a cutaway bundle with measured strands;
- a pier receives an upward force arrow, then appears in aligned old/new designs with stress highlighted;
- the camera leaves the bridge deck and moves underwater to show the construction problem;
- a parking surface opens into the underground volume;
- a rope is isolated, breaks, and becomes part of a historical test.

The viewer sees the mechanism occur rather than reading a label that says it occurred.

### Camera changes correspond to reasoning changes

- aerial or wide views establish the familiar object;
- macro close-ups isolate the hidden component;
- top-down and underwater views expose a route or inaccessible layer;
- cross-sections show what is inside;
- side-by-side frames compare designs at the same scale;
- tracking shots follow the object or construction sequence.

### Text is subordinate to the object

Korean captions are short and centered in a stable lower-middle band. Large labels name the current object or contrast. Measurements and force markers are anchored to the structure. The screen normally has one visual idea, so the 3D object remains readable on a phone.

### Accent color carries causality

Most materials are restrained. A bright accent marks the active cable, direction, stress point, measurement, or misconception. Color says `look here now`; it is not spread evenly across the frame.

### Misconception is removed visually

At least one sampled video uses a large red X over the expected construction method before replacing it with the actual process. Another uses aligned old/new design panels and a highlighted stress region. This gives the explanation a visible before-and-after payoff.

## Adaptation for company and investing stories

Use the same reasoning structure with original assets and brand-aware design:

```text
familiar company image
-> surprising impossible-looking business result
-> enter the product, facility, or contract
-> reveal the hidden layer
-> animate one economic flow at a time
-> compare the common interpretation with the documented structure
-> close on the consequence or identity redefinition
```

For investment-story mode only, add KPI, dated valuation, and verdict after this story resolves.

Examples:

- NVIDIA neocloud: enter a data center, reveal empty versus contracted racks, show customer demand first, then the documented capacity floor, then a separate upside revenue loop.
- AWS Trainium: fly from a data-center campus into one rack, multiply to the documented chip scale, follow a Claude training request through the custom-silicon path, then reveal capex and utilization risk.
- Buybacks: move from a cash pile into repurchased shares, let stock compensation enter from the opposite side, then show the net diluted share count rather than a gross headline.
- Regulatory moat: enter a previously single-lane gate, open a second standards-compliant route, and animate customer flow shifting under explicit assumptions.

## Required motion contract

For every mechanism-led company Short:

1. Name one persistent physical or visual subject.
2. Define at least three explanatory states for it.
3. State the causal fact taught by each state and the supporting claim ID.
4. Assign a camera purpose to each state.
5. Define the transition that preserves spatial continuity.
6. Include one comparison, cutaway, or false-model removal when the story depends on a common misconception.
7. Verify the animation itself does not imply a larger percentage, broader contract, or stronger causality than the ledger supports.
